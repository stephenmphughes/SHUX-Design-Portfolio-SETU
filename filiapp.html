Filí App

A UX case study exploring creative learning tools for the Irish language.

Problem Area

Irish Has Cultural Momentum — but Learners Still Need Better Ways to Use It

Filí began as a way to help Irish learners with two practical language challenges: pronunciation and vocabulary retention. The concept emerged alongside a wider cultural shift in how younger audiences engage with Irish, with contemporary music, film, social media and artists such as Kneecap helping to make the language feel more visible and culturally relevant.

The opportunity was therefore not simply to encourage interest in Irish, but to explore how that interest could be supported by a practical digital tool for using, hearing and remembering the language.

Concept

Why a Rhyming App?

Rhyme offered a way to approach those challenges through sound rather than grammar alone. Filí uses rhyming words to encourage learners to notice pronunciation, syllables, repeated sound patterns and spelling while building vocabulary through active use.

The same tool also serves a creative audience. Poets, songwriters and musicians need words that work structurally and musically, creating a natural overlap between language learning and creative writing.

Filí is designed around Irish, but the underlying concept could later be adapted to other languages where rhyme, rhythm and sound-based learning can support vocabulary and pronunciation.

Research Evidence

Evidence Behind the Problem and the Concept

Research was used to test both sides of the idea: whether there was a real appetite for better ways to engage with Irish, and whether pronunciation, communication, rhyme and rhythm had a credible role in language learning more broadly.

Finding 01

Interest in Irish is strong, but the learning experience is not keeping pace

A Studyclix survey of 1,300 sixth-year students found that 81% believed the way Irish is taught is outdated, even as contemporary Irish-language music and media were helping to increase enthusiasm for the language.

National research published by Údarás na Gaeltachta, Gaelchultúr and Amárach Research found that 74% wanted to improve their Irish and 66% regretted not having better Irish.

Design implication: there is an opportunity to turn renewed cultural interest into practical language use through tools that feel more active, contemporary and engaging than traditional classroom learning alone.

Finding 02

Pronunciation affects confidence, and learners value communication

Pronunciation is not only about producing individual sounds. Stress, rhythm and intonation influence whether speech feels natural and whether learners feel confident using a language aloud.

One EFL pronunciation study found 35.9% identified stress and 31.1% identified intonation as their most difficult pronunciation factors. A separate Universitas Muhammadiyah Semarang study reported that 87.1% agreed that poor pronunciation made them less confident when speaking.

In the University of Raparin study, 67.5% considered communicative classes more significant than grammar.

Design implication: Filí should encourage learners to work with sound, syllables and spoken patterns instead of treating vocabulary as isolated written words.

Finding 03

Rhythm and rhyme can support vocabulary, sound awareness and retention

Research from the University of Cambridge and Trinity College Dublin found that rhythmic information plays an important role in early language development. Professor Usha Goswami describes rhythm as a universal feature of language, with recurring stressed syllables helping listeners detect structure in speech.

“Rhythm is a universal aspect of every language all over the world.”

Professor Usha Goswami — University of Cambridge

An experimental study in Oman involving 60 kindergarten pupils found nursery-rhyme instruction supported vocabulary performance, memorisation, retention, comprehension, motivation and confidence.

The 2025 Nigerian study by Okunola et al. provides a useful experimental comparison: pupils receiving rhyme-based vocabulary instruction improved substantially more than the control group and also achieved higher post-intervention scores in phonological awareness and reading comprehension.

Design implication: rhyme provides a credible foundation for Filí's core interactions: pairing words by sound, repeating patterns, practising spelling and pronunciation, and using vocabulary creatively.

Audience

Target Market

Filí sits between a language-learning tool and a creative writing utility. Its primary audience is people who want to strengthen their Irish vocabulary, pronunciation and confidence; its secondary audience is people actively creating work through Irish.

  • Irish learners: school students, adult learners and people returning to Irish who want more engaging ways to practise vocabulary, spelling, sound and pronunciation.
  • Creative users: poets, songwriters, musicians, spoken-word artists and other writers looking for Irish rhymes and sound-based word discovery.
  • Future education use: teachers and younger learners could use the games and rhyme activities as supplementary practice alongside structured language teaching.
Design

App Design

The research was translated into a set of connected features for sound-based language exploration, creative writing and learning. The design keeps the rhyming dictionary as the core utility while supporting different ways to practise, create and interact with Irish.

Key Features

The feature set moves from word discovery and writing support into personalised learning, gamification and community feedback.

Rhyming Dictionary

The rhyming dictionary is the core focus of the app. Users can search any Irish word and receive a full list of rhyming results pulled from the database. For more precision, results can be filtered by syllable count using the tabbed interface—making it easier to find words that fit the rhythm and flow of a poem, song, or script.

This feature encourages learners and writers to think more structurally and musically about language, supporting both creative expression and language development.

Standard Dictionary

The standard dictionary is a key part of the app, allowing users to quickly look up definitions, spellings, and word usage in context. It’s designed to support both learners and writers by offering clear, accurate explanations in Irish. With built-in search functionality and seamless tab-switching, users can easily find the word they need without interrupting their workflow.

Whether you're checking a meaning or confirming a spelling, the dictionary is always just a click away.

Smart Notepad

Compose poems or lyrics effortlessly with the smart notepad, which suggests rhyming words as you write. Users can customize settings to match specific poetic structures, such as limericks or quatrains, helping them stay within form. A green slider above the text area scrolls horizontally, displaying rhyming suggestions ranked by popularity.

This lets users stay creatively focused on crafting metaphors, similes, and other poetic techniques—without being distracted by rhyme searches.

Onboarding & Personalization

On first login, users are guided through onboarding screens where they select their preferred dialect(s), level of Irish proficiency, and their reasons or goals for using the app. This setup ensures the experience is tailored to their needs. Dialect selection is especially important, as rhymes can vary depending on pronunciation and regional accents—some words may rhyme in one dialect but not in another.

By capturing this information early, the app can provide more accurate rhymes and feedback. Returning users skip onboarding and jump straight in with their saved preferences.

Gamification 1: Rhyming Words

This game presents the user with an image that visually represents or hints at a particular word. The user must then choose the correct rhyming word from four multiple-choice options. Only one of the options rhymes with the word depicted in the image.

While the image may offer visual clues, the multiple-choice answers themselves are designed to challenge the user further—some may look similar or share spellings, encouraging deeper focus on vowel sounds, especially the difference between vowels with and without fadas.

Gamification 2: Pair Matching

This game mode challenges the user to match images based on rhyming sounds. The user is shown 8 images and must correctly identify 4 rhyming pairs to move forward. When images are selected, they’re color-coded and linked with a line of the same color, providing a clear visual cue for each pair.

Once all matches are made, the user can review their choices and press the submit button at the bottom of the screen to check their results.

Gamification 3: Type a Rhyme

In this game mode, the user must type a word that rhymes with the one provided. Accuracy in spelling is crucial—if the answer is incorrect, the user is encouraged to reflect on possible spelling mistakes. The difficulty of each round depends on the number of valid rhyming words available and the complexity or obscurity of those words.

While users can submit any matching word from the database, levels become progressively harder when the rhyme group contains a larger set of possible answers.

Forum & Feedback

Post your work and receive gentle grammar suggestions or pronunciation tips. Users can toggle between “read” and “feedback” views. A micro-community supports creative growth.

Product Structure

Information Architecture

The information architecture brings the key features into one connected structure. The rhyming dictionary remains the central tool, supported by standard word lookup, writing tools, learning games, onboarding and community feedback. Personalisation captures goals, proficiency and dialect preferences so the experience can become more relevant to each user.
